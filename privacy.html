When you fill in the form on the home page, this is what is submitted — nothing is collected quietly in the background beyond the last three lines, which the form fills in for itself:

  • the channel you would like the reply in;

  • the campaign parameters in the link you arrived by (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign), if there were any;
  • the address of the page that referred you to us, if your browser sent one;
  • the time you pressed send.

We ask for this to prepare a written estimate and to open your file. The site never asks for payment details, passport numbers or documents — if those become necessary, they are handled with your concierge directly, not through this page.

Once you order a service, we collect what that service needs, directly from you and from the providers involved:

Veterinarians, laboratories, government agencies, airlines and other providers working on your Pet’s trip send us documents and confirmations that may contain your information.

  • Hosting. The site is served by GitHub Pages. Like any web server, it processes your IP address and basic request data to deliver the page and to keep the service secure.
  • Fonts. Our pages use typefaces from Google Fonts. When they load, your browser contacts Google’s servers (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com) and Google receives your IP address. Whether they load depends on where you are, as described in the next section: in the United States and most of the world they load with the page; in the EU, EEA, UK, Switzerland and Iceland they load only after you choose “Accept”, and until then the page is shown in your system’s own fonts. We would rather serve the fonts ourselves and intend to.
  • No social widgets or chat tools load on any page, in any region.

07Cookies and analytics

The site itself sets no cookies. Three third-party tools do, once they run: Google Analytics 4 and Microsoft Clarity (analytics) and the Meta Pixel (advertising measurement for Facebook and Instagram). They record which pages you open, what you click, your approximate location from your IP address, and your device and browser. Clarity additionally records a playback of pointer movement, scrolling and clicks on the page. The pixel tells Meta that you opened a page and, if you send a request, that a request was sent; we use this to see which ads bring people to us and to show our ads to people likely to need the service.

Whether these tools run before you have said anything depends on where you are. The page reads your browser’s time zone and treats a European time zone (Europe/…, plus Iceland, the Canary Islands, Madeira, the Azores and the Faroe Islands) as the EU mode; every other time zone gets the US mode. The mode is written to your browser’s local storage under the name pw-region so that you, or we, can check which one applied.

  • US mode (opt-out). Analytics and advertising cookies are on when the page opens, and a banner tells you so with two choices: “OK” keeps them on; “Opt out” turns them off on the spot, without a reload — the tools are told that consent is withdrawn, their scripts are removed from the page, and the cookies they set on our domain (_ga, _gid, _gcl, _fbp, _fbc, _clck, _clsk) are deleted. Your choice is kept, so the tools stay off on later visits. The link “Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information” at the bottom of each page opens the same switch at any time.
  • Global Privacy Control. If your browser sends the Global Privacy Control signal, the US mode treats it as an opt-out: nothing loads and the banner is not shown. You can still turn the tools on through the link in the footer; an explicit choice there overrides the signal.
  • EU mode (opt-in). Nothing loads until you answer — no analytics, no advertising tags, and no Google Fonts either; the page is shown in your system’s own fonts. The banner offers “Reject” and “Accept” with equal weight. “Reject” keeps everything off and is remembered. “Accept” loads the three tools and the fonts. “Cookie settings” at the bottom of each page reopens the banner so you can withdraw consent at any time; withdrawing works the same way as “Opt out” above.
  • What stays in your browser. Two values in local storage, never sent to us: your answer under pw-consent (“all” or “essential”) and the mode under pw-region. If you start filling in the request form, a draft of what you typed is kept under pw-draft for 24 hours so a closed tab does not lose it; it is deleted when the request is sent or when you choose “Submit another request”. Clearing site data for this domain in your browser erases all of this together with any analytics and advertising cookies.
  • Consent signals to the tools. Google receives your status through its Consent Mode (analytics, ad storage, ad user data and ad personalization are all “granted” or all “denied” together); Meta receives it through the pixel’s consent setting. The pixel reports a sent request to Meta only while your status is “granted”.
  • Meta Conversions API. When you send a request while your status is “granted”, our server also reports that request to Meta directly, with your email address and phone number in hashed form (a one-way code Meta uses to match an account, not readable text), your browser type and the identifiers of the ad you clicked. Your route, your pet’s details and your message are not sent to Meta. With status “denied” none of this is sent.

Our website does not respond to the older “Do Not Track” browser setting; it does respond to Global Privacy Control as described above. The FAQ, Terms and this Privacy page carry no analytics at all; in the EU mode they also leave the fonts off unless you have accepted on another page of this site.

  • Your passport or ID details, booking references and frequent-flyer numbers — for up to twelve (12) months after the services in your Order are complete, so that we can serve your future Orders, and then we delete them.
  • Your Order, invoices, task log, documents and correspondence — for up to seven (7) years after the services are complete, as business and tax records and in case of a dispute.
  • A request sent through the website that does not become an Order — while we are in touch with you about it, and afterwards only for our own records.
  • Photos used in marketing — until you withdraw your consent.
  • Website logs — for the short period needed for security and troubleshooting.

We keep information longer if the law requires it or if it is needed for a pending claim or dispute. If you would like something removed sooner, write to us and we will do it, except where the law requires us to keep a record.

Information we handle while working on your Order is kept in password-protected storage, with access limited to the personnel who need it. No arrangement on the internet is perfectly secure, and we would rather say so than promise otherwise. If a security incident affects your personal information, we will notify you as required by law, including under Florida’s Information Protection Act (Fla. Stat. § 501.171).
