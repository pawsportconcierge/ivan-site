How early should you start preparing your pet’s flight?

15 August 2026 Pawsport Concierge desk

There is no single answer, because the answer belongs to the route rather than to the calendar. What we can say is when the work actually starts — and it is earlier than most people expect.

The short answer

Message us the day you start thinking about the trip. Not the day you book, and not the day you find a flight you like — the day the idea appears. Many routes need about 30 days; some countries require blood tests months in advance. Until we know the destination, the airline and your pet, any number would be a guess, and guesses are what go wrong in pet travel.

We run the calendar backwards from your date and tell you immediately whether it is realistic.

Why the calendar runs backwards

Every document your pet needs is valid only inside its own window. Travel papers expire in days, not weeks. A test done too early stops counting; a stamp collected too late arrives after the flight has gone. So the timeline is not built forwards from today — it is built backwards from the departure date, so that each step lands inside the window that accepts it.

That is also why the earliest question is rarely “how long does it take.” It is “which of these windows is already closing.”

What has to be settled before you book a ticket

Booking first and sorting the paperwork afterwards is the most common and most expensive order of operations. Before a ticket is paid for, these are the things that decide whether the trip is possible at all:

  • The destination country’s rules for your pet, read line by line rather than from a summary.
  • The airline’s written confirmation for your specific route, pet and crate.
  • Whether your pet travels in the cabin — always our first option where the airline allows it — or in the hold, agreed with you in advance.
  • The order and timing of vet visits, government stamps and endorsements.

Once those are known, the flight can be chosen with confidence instead of hope.

What you can find out in the first fifteen minutes

You do not need to prepare anything to ask. Send the route, the approximate dates and what kind of pet is travelling. A named concierge replies within 15 minutes, 8am–10pm ET, every day, with a plan for your route and a fixed written quote — before anything is paid for.

If the date you have in mind is not realistic, you will hear that in the same reply. That is more useful than an encouraging answer, and it is the reason to ask early rather than late.

Questions we get on this

How early should we start preparing a pet for a flight?

Message us the day you start thinking about the trip. Many routes need about 30 days; some countries require blood tests months in advance. We run the calendar backwards from your date and tell you immediately whether it is realistic.

What if the airline or the border refuses my pet?

That is exactly what we prevent. Your route is confirmed in writing with the airline before you book a ticket, and country requirements are cleared line by line. We do not gamble with departure day.

Can my pet fly in the cabin with me?

Whenever the airline allows it — yes, and that is always our first option. It depends on the airline, the route and your pet’s size. The cargo hold is a last resort, and only after we have agreed it with you.
